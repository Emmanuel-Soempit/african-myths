History & Origins

expand_more

The Yoruba people are one of the largest ethnic groups in Africa. Their history is long and rich, with its origins often traced back to the city of Ile-Ife in present-day Nigeria, which is considered the cradle of Yoruba civilization. According to Yoruba mythology, Ile-Ife was founded by the deity Oduduwa, from whom all Yoruba people are said to descend.

By the 11th century, the Yoruba had established a powerful and complex society organized into city-states. The most notable of these was the Oyo Empire, which rose to prominence in the 17th century and became one of the largest West African states. The Oyo Empire was known for its sophisticated political structure, powerful cavalry, and extensive trade networks that reached across the Sahara.

Yoruba art, particularly its naturalistic bronze and terracotta sculptures from Ife and Benin, is celebrated worldwide for its technical skill and aesthetic beauty. These artistic traditions, along with their complex religious and philosophical systems, have profoundly influenced African and diaspora cultures, particularly in the Americas through the transatlantic slave trade.

Language

expand_more

The Yorùbá language is a Niger-Congo language spoken in West Africa, primarily in Southwestern and Central Nigeria. It is spoken by the ethnic Yoruba people. The number of speakers of Yoruba is estimated at over 40 million. It is a tonal language with three tones: high, mid, and low. The standard variety of the language, often called "Yoruba Koiné," is based on the dialect of the Oyo Empire.
