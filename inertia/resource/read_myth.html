How Anansi Became the Keeper of Stories

schedule 7 min read

Trickery

Wisdom

Creation

In the beginning, there were no stories in the world. The Sky-God, Nyame, had them all, kept safe in a golden box beside his throne. Anansi the Spider, a clever and cunning trickster, yearned for these stories. He wanted to bring them down to the people of the earth. So, he spun a long, silver thread and climbed up to the sky to speak with Nyame.

"Nyame," Anansi said with a bow, "I wish to buy your stories."

The Sky-God laughed. "Many have tried and failed, little spider. The price is high. You must bring me three things: Mmoboro, the hornets who sting like fire; Onini, the great python who can swallow a man whole; and Osebo, the leopard with teeth like daggers."

Anansi did not despair. He returned to Earth and first went to the hornets' nest. "They say you are fools," he taunted, holding a large, empty calabash. "They say you cannot fly into this small gourd." Angered, the hornets swarmed inside to prove him wrong, and Anansi quickly sealed the opening.

With cunning and wit, Anansi showed that strength is not always measured in size, but in the sharpness of the mind.

Next, he found Onini the python. "My wife and I disagree," Anansi said. "She says you are not as long as this palm branch." The proud python stretched himself out against the branch, and Anansi swiftly tied him to it with strong creepers.

Finally, for Osebo the leopard, Anansi dug a deep pit. When the leopard fell in, Anansi offered to help him out using his web. But as he pulled the leopard up, he tied him to a tree. With all three captives, Anansi returned to the Sky-God. Nyame was amazed. He declared, "Anansi, you have paid the price. From this day, all stories belong to you. They shall be called 'Spider Stories'." And that is how Anansi became the keeper of stories, sharing them with all the people of the world.

Source: A traditional Akan folktale, retold for Kólà.
